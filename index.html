On AI

I learned to code before AI. Now I build with it every day.

AI is part of how I work

I use AI tools throughout my projects and day-to-day work, and I enjoy it. They help me move faster, try ideas I wouldn't have had time for, and keep my code in line with modern standards. It's one of the most exciting shifts I've seen in this industry.

Grounded in fundamentals

I taught myself web development through The Odin Project in 2020, before AI coding tools went mainstream. Knowing how to read docs, debug by hand and understand why code works is what lets me get real value out of AI, and as a QA engineer I test and own everything I ship.

A fan of the tech, not the companies

I'm not a fan of the ethics of many of the companies behind these products. I think it's worth being clear-eyed about that, and it doesn't change how useful the technology is or how much I like working with it.

Projects

Mobile and web app · React Native · Coming soon to the App Store and Google Play

Book Club

A cross-platform app for small reading groups. Create a club, invite your friends, and read the same book together, with everyone's progress, meetings and next picks in one place. It's headed to the App Store and Google Play soon.

  • Shared reading progress: each member logs their own page, and it resets when the club moves to a new book.
  • Schedule meetings with a date, time and note, and keep a list of the books the club has finished.
  • Members suggest books and owners choose the next read. Search uses Open Library, with a "wrong book?" picker for alternate editions.
  • Email invites that open straight into the app, or can be pasted in as a link. Sign in with email or look around as a guest first.
  • Private by default: members appear by display name only, and ownership passes on when the only owner leaves.
  • Built-in support contact form and full account deletion from Settings, backed by Appwrite Functions.

Discord bot · Node.js

Barkwar Toothbeard

A Discord bot that lets Magic: The Gathering players share the cards they're hunting for, so trades are easier to find within a server.

  • Checks every card name against the Scryfall API, skips unknown names and suggests the likely match.
  • Accepts messy pasted lists and strips bullets and quantities automatically.
  • Stores player profiles, want lists and binder links in Firebase Firestore.
  • Per-user rate limiting, plus automated tests and syntax checks in GitHub Actions.
